EGC Games Specification Document
Overview
This document details the games to be implemented in the EGC Web App for team competition duringthe conference. All games are designed with the following principles:
1.
Audio-only communication
in the Main Game Room
2.
Direct interaction
with the game field via typing, touching, or drawing
3.
Objective scoring
with clear right/wrong outcomes
4.
Turn-based gameplay
with automated microphone management
5.
Automated validation
of answers without subjective judging
6.
Fair competition
with equal opportunity for all teams
Game Format
Each team plays multiple rounds of each game
Games have allocated time limits (typically 5-10 minutes per game session)
Team with the highest point total across their rounds is declared the winner
Points are tracked and accumulated across all games for overall conference standings
Selected Games
1. Tic-Tac-Toe
Game Mechanics:
Classic 3x3 grid with X and O markers
Teams alternate turns placing their marker
First to form a row of three markers (horizontal, vertical, or diagonal) wins the round
Best of 5 rounds determines the winner
Interaction Method:
Teams use touch/click interaction to select grid position for marker placement
Grid cells light up when hovered to indicate potential selection
Scoring:
1 point for each round won
Bonus point for winning 3 consecutive rounds
Maximum possible score: 6 points
Time Allocation:
30 seconds per turn
8 minutes maximum per match (to accommodate multiple rounds)
Use Case Example:
Team A and Team B are playing. The system displays a 3x3 grid. When it's Team A'sturn, their microphones activate for discussion. A designated Team A player clicks on their chosenposition, and an X appears. Team A's mics are then muted, and Team B's activate. Team B clicks theirchosen position for an O. This continues until Team A connects three Xs diagonally and wins the round.The system records the win, resets the board, and starts round 2 with Team B going first.
2. Sudoku Relay
Game Mechanics:
9x9 grid partially filled with numbers
Teams take turns filling in one cell per turn
System validates each entry immediately
First team to correctly complete their assigned cells wins
Interaction Method:
Teams click/tap on empty cell and type number (1-9)
Submit button to confirm entry
Scoring:
1 point for each correctly placed number
-1 point for incorrect entries
5 bonus points for completing an entire row, column, or 3x3 box
Maximum possible score varies by puzzle difficulty
Time Allocation:
45 seconds per turn
10 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
A 9x9 Sudoku grid appears with about 40% filled. Team C's microphones activate fortheir turn. After discussion, a team member clicks on an empty cell and types "7". When they click submit,the system validates the entry. The cell turns green, confirming the correct placement, and 1 point isawarded. If they had completed a row with this entry, they would receive 5 bonus points. Play passes toTeam D who selects another empty cell and enters a number.
3. Hangman with Biblical Terms
Game Mechanics:
Hidden biblical words or phrases represented by blank spaces
Teams guess one letter at a time
Correct guesses reveal letter positions; incorrect guesses build the hangman figure
Team must solve the word before the hangman is complete (typically 6 incorrect guesses)
Interaction Method:
Teams type their letter guess in an input field
Alternatively, they can click/tap on an on-screen alphabet
Scoring:
1 point for each correct letter guessed
5 bonus points for solving the complete word/phrase
-1 point for each incorrect guess
Maximum possible score depends on word length
Time Allocation:
30 seconds per turn
6 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
The system displays "_ _ _ _ _ _
" (7 blanks). Team B's microphones activate, and theydiscuss which letter to guess. A team member types "E" and submits. The display updates to "
E _ _ _ E _"revealing two Es. Team B gets another turn because they guessed correctly. They guess "R" which isn't inthe word, so part of the hangman is drawn. Team B's mics mute and Team C's activate for their turn. Thiscontinues until either the word "BETHANY" is revealed or the hangman is completed.
4. Crossword Puzzle Race
Game Mechanics:
Biblical-themed crossword puzzle with clues
Teams take turns selecting and answering clues
System validates answers immediately
Points awarded based on correct answers and word length
Interaction Method:
Teams click/tap on a clue number to select it
Type answer in the corresponding cells
Submit button to confirm answer
Scoring:
1 point per letter in correctly answered words
3 bonus points for completing intersecting words simultaneously
No penalty for incorrect answers, but no points awarded
Maximum possible score depends on puzzle size
Time Allocation:
45 seconds per turn
12 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
A biblical crossword puzzle appears. Team D selects clue "5-Across: King who builtthe Temple" by clicking on number 5. The corresponding cells are highlighted for input. After discussion,a team member types "SOLOMON" and submits. The system validates this as correct, fills in the letters ingreen, and awards 7 points. If this answer completed a connecting down word as well, they would receive3 bonus points. Play passes to the next team who selects another clue.
5. Word Chain
Game Mechanics:
Starting with a biblical word, teams add words that begin with the last letter of the previous word
All words must be related to biblical/church themes
System validates word legitimacy and connection
No repetition of words allowed
Interaction Method:
Teams type their word in an input field
Submit button to confirm entry
Scoring:
Points equal to the length of valid words (e.g., "PSALM" = 5 points)
Double points for words longer than 7 letters
No points for invalid or repeated words
Maximum score depends on team creativity and biblical knowledge
Time Allocation:
40 seconds per turn
8 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
The game begins with the word "GOSPEL". Team A must provide a word starting with"L". Their microphones activate and after discussion, a team member types "LOCUSTS" and submits. Thesystem validates this as acceptable and awards 7 points. Team B must now provide a word starting with"S", and they submit "SAMSON" for 6 points. This continues until a team cannot think of a valid wordwithin the time limit or provides an invalid entry.
6. Bible Verse Completion
Game Mechanics:
System displays partial Bible verses with missing words
Teams must complete the verse by typing the missing words
Automated scoring based on accuracy
Progressive difficulty levels
Interaction Method:
Teams type the missing text in provided blank fields
Submit button to confirm answer
Scoring:
10 points for exact completion
5 points for semantically correct but not exact wording
0 points for incorrect completions
Bonus points for difficult verses (3-5 points)
Maximum possible score varies by verse selection
Time Allocation:
45 seconds per turn
10 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
The system displays: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begottenSon, that whosoever _________ in him should not perish, but have everlasting life." Team C's microphonesactivate, and they discuss the missing word. A team member types "believeth" and submits. The systemchecks against the correct answer and awards 10 points for an exact match. If they had typed "believes,"they might receive 5 points for being semantically correct. Play passes to the next team with a new verse.
7. Word Blitz
Game Mechanics:
Set of 9-12 random letters displayed
Teams form as many biblical/church-related words as possible
Minimum 3-letter words
Dictionary validation ensures fairness
Interaction Method:
Teams type words in rapid succession in an input field
Submit button after each word
Word list displays all valid words found
Scoring:
1 point for 3-letter words
2 points for 4-letter words
3 points for 5-letter words
5 points for 6+ letter words
Maximum score depends on letter combination and team speed
Time Allocation:
60 seconds per team turn (all words must be submitted within this time)
8 minutes maximum per game (to allow all teams to play)
Use Case Example:
The system displays the letters "SEPTNHAOMS". Team B's microphones activate, andthey have 60 seconds to find as many words as possible. Team members call out words while adesignated person types and submits them rapidly: "AMEN" (1 point), "STONE" (2 points), "PATHS" (2points), "HOPE" (1 point), "ANTHEM" (3 points). The system validates each word against a biblicaldictionary in real-time. After 60 seconds, their words and final score are displayed, and play passes toTeam C with a new set of letters.
8. Name That Tune - Hymns Edition
Game Mechanics:
Short clips (5-10 seconds) of well-known hymns or worship songs played
Teams must identify the song title and/or artist
Multiple difficulty levels (easy, medium, hard)
Automated matching against correct answers
Interaction Method:
Teams type their answer in an input field after hearing the clip
Submit button to confirm answer
Scoring:
5 points for correctly identifying song title
3 additional points for correctly identifying artist/composer
Bonus points for difficult songs (1-5 points)
Maximum possible score varies by song selection
Time Allocation:
20 seconds to answer after clip plays
8 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
The system plays a 7-second audio clip of a hymn. Team D's microphones activate,and they discuss what they just heard. A team member types "Amazing Grace - John Newton" andsubmits. The system verifies the answer, awarding 5 points for the correct song title and 3 points for thecorrect composer. The game continues with different hymn clips for each team, with points talliedthroughout.
9. Bible Trivia Blitz
Game Mechanics:
Rapid-fire biblical questions with multiple choice answers
Questions appear one at a time
Teams answer as many questions as possible within time limit
Progressive difficulty increases point values
Interaction Method:
Teams click/tap on their chosen answer (A, B, C, or D)
Next button appears after each answer to progress
Scoring:
1 point for easy questions
2 points for medium questions
3 points for difficult questions
No penalty for incorrect answers
Maximum score depends on number of questions answered correctly
Time Allocation:
90 seconds per team to answer as many questions as possible
10 minutes maximum per game (allowing all teams to participate)
Use Case Example:
Team A's microphones activate and a 90-second timer begins. Questions appear oneafter another: "Who was the first king of Israel?" with options displayed. A team member clicks "B. Saul"which is correct, earning 1 point. The next question appears immediately. Team A answers as manyquestions as possible within their time limit. When time expires, their final score is displayed, and Team Bbegins their 90-second round with different questions.
10. Scripture Scramble
Game Mechanics:
Words from a Bible verse are scrambled/out of order
Teams must arrange words in correct order to form the verse
System validates the arrangement automatically
Multiple difficulty levels based on verse length and complexity
Interaction Method:
Teams drag and drop words into correct positions
Submit button to check arrangement
Scoring:
10 points for completely correct arrangement
5 points for partially correct arrangement (> 75% correct)
2 points for minimally correct arrangement (50-75% correct)
Bonus points for difficult verses (3-5 points)
Maximum possible score varies by verse selection
Time Allocation:
60 seconds per turn
8 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
The system displays scrambled words: "life the am I resurrection and the". Team C'smicrophones activate, and they discuss the correct order. A designated team member drags and dropsthe words to form "I am the resurrection and the life". They submit their arrangement, and the systemverifies it as correct, awarding 10 points. Play passes to the next team with a new scrambled verse.
11. Bible Categories
Game Mechanics:
Categories related to the Bible displayed (e.g., Books, Prophets, Women in the Bible)
Teams must provide valid entries for the given category
No repetition allowed (across all teams)
System validates entries against a comprehensive database
Interaction Method:
Teams type their entry in an input field
Submit button to confirm entry
Scoring:
1 point for each valid entry
3 bonus points for obscure/uncommon entries (predetermined by system)
No points for invalid or repeated entries
Maximum score depends on biblical knowledge and creativity
Time Allocation:
30 seconds per turn
10 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
The category "Women in the Bible" is displayed. Team A's microphones activate, andthey discuss potential answers. A team member types "Ruth" and submits. The system validates this ascorrect and awards 1 point. Team B follows with "Esther" for 1 point. Later in the game, Team D submits"Jael" which is both correct and considered uncommon, earning them 1+3=4 points. Play continues untilteams exhaust their knowledge or time runs out.
12. Connect the Dots - Bible Stories
Game Mechanics:
Series of events from a Bible story presented out of order
Teams must connect events in the correct chronological sequence
Multiple stories to play through
System validates connections automatically
Interaction Method:
Teams drag lines to connect events in order
Alternatively, teams number the events sequentially
Submit button to check sequence
Scoring:
10 points for completely correct sequence
1 point deducted for each event out of place
Bonus points for difficult stories (3-5 points)
Maximum possible score varies by story complexity
Time Allocation:
45 seconds per turn
8 minutes maximum per game
Use Case Example:
The system displays events from David's life: "Defeats Goliath", "Anointed bySamuel", "Plays harp for Saul", "Becomes king", "Spares Saul's life". Team B's microphones activate, andthey discuss the correct order. A team member numbers or connects these events in the order theybelieve is correct. They submit their sequence, and the system compares it to the correct order. If theyplaced 4 out of 5 events correctly, they would receive 10-1=9 points. Play passes to the next team withevents from another Bible story.
Technical Implementation Notes
Game Interface Requirements
1.
Main Game Room Interface:
Clean, distraction-free design
Clear indication of whose turn it is
Prominent timer display
Team score counters
Turn history log
2.
Common Room Interface:
Mirror of game progress in real-time
Live chat sidebar for spectators
Team standings and statistics
Upcoming game schedule
Input Methods
All games must support:
Touch/click interactions for mobile and desktop
Keyboard input for text-based games
Drag-and-drop functionality where applicable
Clear submit/confirm buttons for all actions
Accessibility Considerations
Color-blind friendly visual indicators (not relying solely on color)
Keyboard navigation options
Screen reader compatibility for text elements
Adequate text size and contrast
Technical Validation
Server-side validation for all answers to prevent cheating
Biblical dictionary and knowledge base for answer checking
Response time monitoring to ensure fair play
Backup scoring system in case of technical issues
Scheduling and Points System
Game Session Format
Each game played in rotation
All teams play same game before moving to next game
2-3 games per conference day
Final day features championship games with top teams
Overall Scoring
Points from all games accumulated throughout conference
Daily leaderboard updates
Team rankings displayed prominently
Trophy/recognition for overall conference champion
Tie-Breaking System
In case of tied scores:
1.
Head-to-head results
2.
Performance in "Bible Verse Completion" (designated tie-breaker game)
3.
Total correct answers across all games
4.
Total time used (less time is better)
